Will this land me a job? The straight answer

No employment outcome is guaranteed. A completed, tested project can be portfolio evidence, but this unbuilt HTML guide is not evidence of a working system, a reference, or interview fluency.

Job Bank groups the data engineer profile under Data scientists (NOC 21211) and rates Alberta prospects Moderate. GoA Systems Analyst is a separate classification; its current Level 1 MRS accepts a related two-year diploma, or a related one-year certificate/qualifying industry certification plus one year of related experience. Experience alone does not satisfy that written education/certification route. The specific job posting and hiring ministry decide relatedness.

Honest next step: finish and test the system before describing it as working. Publish source code, test results, limitations and flight records; then use it as one part of a broader job application rather than a substitute for required education or experience.
What it can and cannot see (the honest capability line)

Design target, not a verified capability: test a controlled warm target from low altitude and record detection rate, false positives and geolocation error. At 30 m AGL, a 55°×35° frame is about 31×20 m, or roughly 1×0.8 m per pixel; a 1 m target may occupy about one pixel and cannot be promised detectable. Day/night, emissivity, atmospheric conditions, motion, tilt and terrain all affect results.

Cannot: replace agency aircraft or declare an area safe. The MLX90640's 32×24 grid is a learning-grade sensor. No detection range, mapping accuracy, professional sensor cost, flight endurance or operational readiness claim is bench-verified for this build.

The one legal line that never bends: no drone may fly over or within 5 nautical miles (9.3 km) of a wildfire below 3,000 ft (CAR 601.15). Real fires are agency airspace — unauthorised drones ground air tankers and endanger crews. You will train on safe heat targets: fire pits, burn barrels, hot plates — on private land, with permission. That is exactly how professionals calibrate their gear too.
Why a drone beats the ground station for your portfolio

An airborne prototype can combine mechanical, radio, sensing, positioning and software work, but it is not inherently more valuable than a tested ground station. FPInnovations' Alberta-funded 2020 report calls the platform only one small part of the solution and says the mission is incomplete until interpreted data reaches the firefighter as a map of GPS-referenced hotspots. That supports the value of integration and reporting, not a hiring guarantee.

What you'll be able to say when it's true
"I built a thermal-camera drone from parts — assembled the airframe, configured the flight controller, wrote and tested the hotspot-detection code, and flew night detection missions over safe targets. It finds a 1-metre heat source from 30 metres and puts it on a map with GPS coordinates. I can explain every part of it. Want to see?"

Ideal nadir formulas: ground width = 2 × altitude × tan(FOV/2); horizontal metres/pixel = width/32. At 100 m, the 55°×35° lens gives approximately 104.1×63.1 m and 3.25×2.63 m per pixel. Geographic conversion uses ~111,320 m/degree latitude and longitude scaling by cos(latitude), but omits tilt, terrain, lens distortion and GPS/heading/height error.

🔥 What "detecting a fire" actually means

The payload pipeline has three software stages, followed by hardware validation:

  1. Sense: the MLX90640 returns 768 temperatures per frame. A fire is a cluster of hot pixels — say 38 °C background, 300+ °C at the target.
  2. Detect: the included hotspots.py flood-fills edge-connected hot pixels into groups with centroid, size and peak temperature. Synthetic tests cover grouping, thresholds, edges and invalid inputs.
  3. Report: the included geo.py converts pixels using position, altitude and heading; payload.py logs results and map_server.py displays them offline. Detection and coordinate accuracy still require controlled hardware tests.

The same three layers work from the ground, the air, or a satellite feed — that's why the pipeline is the career asset, not the airframe.

⏱ Where & when you may fly (verified TC rules)

Basic operations — what this build flies under (TC page updated 19 Mar 2026):

  • Operating weight no more than 25 kg; measure the completed aircraft rather than relying on the removed 1 kg/3.7 kg estimates. Maintain VLOS.
  • More than 30 m horizontally from any person. This is an operations-category boundary, not a universal safe distance.
  • Uncontrolled airspace, more than 5.6 km from the centre of a certified airport, more than 1.9 km from the centre of a heliport; military-aerodrome permission is separate.
  • Register and mark the drone (250 g to 25 kg) and carry proof plus a Pilot Certificate — Basic Operations. The Small Basic exam and registration have fees; age 14+ applies to Basic. The certificate does not expire, but skills must stay current.
Night lighting is necessary, not sufficient (CAR 901.39): the lights must be on and sufficient for the pilot or a visual observer to see the aircraft. A generic LED kit does not by itself make a flight legal; VLOS, airspace, bystander, aerodrome, NOTAM, site and all other applicable rules still apply.
Forest-fire restriction (CAR 601.15): do not operate over a forest-fire area or within 5 nautical miles (9.3 km) of one below 3,000 ft AGL, and do not enter airspace restricted by a NOTAM under 601.16. Check current NOTAMs, Alberta fire bans and local/site rules before flight. Use a hot plate for bench tests; an open test fire also requires landowner permission and every applicable permit.

  • Order by retailer: the airframe, radio, FPV and most tools come from Rotor Village in Manitoba; the Pi, thermal board and GPS come from PiShop in Ottawa; the Qwiic HAT/cables come from SparkFun; the laptop and small supplies come from Walmart/Edmonton pickup or delivery. Exact links are in the order document.
  • Freight is separate: Rotor Village and PiShop calculate by weight and destination. SparkFun freight/import charges and any small-Walmart-item charge need checkout quotes. Stock is checked, not reserved.
  • Cold-weather reality (Edmonton winter): LiPos lose punch below −10 °C — keep packs warm in a jacket pocket, expect shorter flights, and never charge a frozen pack. The MLX90640 itself is rated to well below freezing.
  • Compliance first: register the drone ($5) and pass the Transport Canada Small Basic online exam (14+) this week — costs almost nothing and it's the piece that makes every flight after it legal.
Order of operations so nothing stalls: radio first (you can practice on a free simulator like Liftoff while everything else ships), then stack + motors + frame (Weekend 1), then Pi + camera (Weekend 2). The exam and registration can be done tonight.
Buying notes & LiPo safety
  • Soldering: airframe wiring needs it. The payload is solder-free only with a pre-populated Pi header/Qwiic HAT; a standard Zero 2 W needs a header soldered. Budget for a temperature-controlled iron, stand, solder, flux, eye protection, ventilation, cutters, strippers, drivers, meter and smoke stopper — not just a bare iron.
  • LiPo safety: never charge unattended, charge in a LiPo bag, never fly a swollen or damaged pack. This is the one part of the hobby that genuinely bites.
  • The Pi + camera are the payload — if you already ordered them for the old ground-station plan, tick them off.

Payload example — included package, 19 synthetic tests passed

import time, board, busio
import adafruit_mlx90640
from hotspots import find_hotspots   # included tested module in Shad-Drone-Payload

i2c = busio.I2C(board.SCL, board.SDA, frequency=800000)
mlx = adafruit_mlx90640.MLX90640(i2c)
mlx.refresh_rate = adafruit_mlx90640.RefreshRate.REFRESH_2_HZ

frame = [0] * 768
while True:
    try:
        mlx.getFrame(frame)
    except ValueError:
        continue
    for h in find_hotspots(frame, 60, 3):   # threshold 60C, 3+ pixels
        print("HOTSPOT,%.1f,%.1f,%d,%.1f" %
              (h["x"], h["y"], h["pixels"], h["max_c"]))
    time.sleep(0.5)

The MLX90640 uses the documented getFrame API, 768-value buffer and refresh-rate settings at address 0x33. The full GPS/logging/map implementation is in Shad-Drone-Payload. Its 19 synthetic tests and demo passed; physical Pi/sensor integration and airborne accuracy still require the listed acceptance tests.
